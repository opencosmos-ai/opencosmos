# 乾 — `sky`

**The rendering is the image, not the action.** 說卦 gives every trigram two glosses: an action
in chapter 7 (乾，…也) and an image in chapter 11 (乾為天). The 大象傳 bound into
every hexagram operates on the **images** — across the 64 they name 天 18 times, 地 17, 雷 15,
山 15, 澤 15, 水 11, 風 10, 火 10, against 健 once, 順 twice, 入 twice, 麗 once, and 動 陷 止 說
not at all. Rendering by image is not promoting one Wing's gloss over another; it is the reading
the vendored text runs on. See [`../WORKLIST.md`](../WORKLIST.md) § A1.

**乾 is the one trigram whose English was already decided elsewhere.** Its 說卦 image is 天,
and 天 is locked by the parent project's glossary to *sky* as a tier and *nature / the natural*
as an agent, with **Heaven, Providence, the Almighty and the Cosmos forbidden** — 122
occurrences in this book. 天地 is locked separately to *sky and earth*. So the work here is
not choosing a word; it is checking that the locked word survives being a trigram name.

It does. 天 appears in 18 of the 64 大象傳, more than any other image, and every one of them
reads as a physical tier: 天與水違行 (訟), 天下有風 (姤), 風行天上 (小畜). None of them needs
a deity, and reading one in is precisely the overlay the lock exists to stop.

**McClatchie writes "Heaven" 18 times**, which is the single clearest demonstration in the
corpus of what the lock is for — and he is not being careless. His Appendix argues at length
that 神 *"signifies… God, Gods"*, and his Book IV renders 帝 as "The (Supreme) Emperor" in one
chapter and "God" in the next. The word is load-bearing for a cosmology this project does not
share.

**The seam.** *Sky* is thinner than 乾. The trigram is three solid lines, the most yang figure
there is, and 說卦 ch 7 glosses it 健 — vigour, unresting strength — which "sky" does not carry.
That loss is accepted here because the alternative is worse: rendering the *action* would make
every 大象傳 unreadable, and rendering "heaven" would import the thing the lock forbids. The
vigour belongs in the hexagram-level notes, not in the one word on the card.

## What is ruled out, and why

Each for what the word does. None for who wrote it — see [[convergence-is-evidence]].

- **"Heaven", "the heavens", "the Almighty"** — they import a deity 天 does not carry. This is
  not a local judgement: 天 is **locked** by the parent glossary across 122 occurrences, and the
  lock names the harm. It would hold if no missionary had ever written the word.
- **"the Creative"** — renders the **action** (說卦 ch 7: 健) where the 大象傳 stack **images**,
  so 天與水違行 (訟) becomes a creativity moving contrary to water. It also imports creation *ex
  nihilo*, which 乾 does not assert.
