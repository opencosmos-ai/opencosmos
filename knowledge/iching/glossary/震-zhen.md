# 震 — `thunder`

**The rendering is the image, not the action.** 說卦 gives every trigram two glosses: an action
in chapter 7 (震，…也) and an image in chapter 11 (震為雷). The 大象傳 bound into
every hexagram operates on the **images** — across the 64 they name 天 18 times, 地 17, 雷 15,
山 15, 澤 15, 水 11, 風 10, 火 10, against 健 once, 順 twice, 入 twice, 麗 once, and 動 陷 止 說
not at all. Rendering by image is not promoting one Wing's gloss over another; it is the reading
the vendored text runs on. See [`../WORKLIST.md`](../WORKLIST.md) § A1.

**The clearest of the eight.** 雷 is thunder, in 15 of the 64 大象傳 — 雷雨作 (解), 雷風 (恆),
雷在天上 (大壯) — and McClatchie writes Thunder 19 times. No lock touches it, the three corners
do not split, and there is no live alternative.

**This is the entry that the old wording of `method.md` would have blocked.** Until 2026-09-12
the fourth corner read *"Never for a word"*, which taken literally forbids *thunder* because
Legge and McClatchie reached it first. That is avoidance, not originality: it would push the
rendering away from the Chinese to buy distance from a translator. The rule now reads *never
for a rendering*, and a term of art is not a rendering. 雷 is thunder.

**The seam.** 說卦 ch 7 glosses 震 動 — movement, arousal — and ch 11 adds 龍, dragon, and
長子, eldest son. Thunder is the image and it is what the 大象傳 stacks; the movement is
carried in the hexagram notes.
