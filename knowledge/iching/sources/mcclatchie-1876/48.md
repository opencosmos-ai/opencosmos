# Hexagram 48 · 井 — McClatchie 1876

> He calls a hexagram a **Diagram**, renders 君子 as **"the Model Man"**, and attributes
> 彖 to "Wăn Wang" and 象 to "Chow Kung". The Chinese faces this on scan pages 259, 261 and is not transcribed:
> see `chinese-pages.yaml`.

## The judgment

The Tsing Diagram implics the impossibility of re-_ ‘moving the well’ when a city is removed; (in this) nothing is lost and nothing is gained, aud -both those who come and those who go, all obtain the well water. When coming to draw water, if the rope is not let down sufficicntly low,’ the pitcher will be broken; this is unlucky. -

## 彖傳 — "Wăn Wang says"

Win Wang says :—A wooden bucket ⟦trigram figure⟧ in the Water ⟦trigram figure⟧ and Water ascending (in the bucket) constitute the Tsing Diagram. A well nourishes without limit—A city can be “removed | ‘but not a well, implies that the Hard has attained the due Medium.’ Approaching to draw water, and not letting down the rope, sufficiently low, implies the absence of merit (in the petitioner for favour), and the breaking of the pitcher is the unluckiness which ensues,

## 大象傳 — "Chow Kung says"

Chow Kung says :—Water ⟦trigram figure⟧ above Wood ⟦trigram figure⟧ is the Tsing Diagram. By it the Model _Man_ rewards the people : ‘and | exhorts them to help ot one 1¢ another.

## The lines

**First-Six.** Represents a well which is muddy and unuseable; an old well and no birds in it. Chow Kung says :—The well being muddy and unuseable refers to the bottom of it.’ An old well without Birds in it, implies loss of opportunity. 1. A “well represents tho favour of thoso in power. 2, Seeking favour improperly. 3. That is, its efficacy as the superior Principle is lasting. 4. No ability to perform duty. 5. That is, to inferior persons employed in the State.

**Second-Nine.** Represents, water dropping on a shrimp from a cavity in a well;* or water leaking from a broken pitcher.* Chow Kung says :—Water dropping on a shrimp from a cavity in a well, implies the absence of friendly intercourse.

**Third-Nine.** Represents a well cleansed but unused,” thus causing distress to the mind; unremitting effort should be used, and if the King is intelligent he will both receive and confer happiness, Chow Kung says :—When the well is cleansed but not used, those who pass by are grieved; good luck is attained in petitioning an intelligent King.

**Fourth-Six.** Is, a well built up with tiles;‘ no blame arises.. Chow Kung says :—When a well is built up with tiles no blame arises, implies that the well is properly repaired.§

**Fifth-Nine.** Represents a well of pure water;° or, drinking from a cool spring. Chow Kung says :—Drinking at a cool spring, implies attaining the undeflected due Medium.

**Sixth-Six.** Signifies, taking water from a well which is not covered up;" inspiring confidence now, is a very fountain of good luck. Chow Kung says:—A very fountain of good luck, refers to the upper classes, and implies great extension of their virtues. 1. One who befriends the poor, although not in office. 2. One powerless from lowly position. 8. A person of talent, yet not promoted. 4. Performing duty, but having no extra merit. 5. That in, virtue is cultivated. 6. A model sovereign. 7.. Receiving benefit from an inexhaustilile gource.
