# Hexagram 14 · 大有 — McClatchie 1876

> He calls a hexagram a **Diagram**, renders 君子 as **"the Model Man"**, and attributes
> 彖 to "Wăn Wang" and 象 to "Chow Kung". The Chinese faces this on scan pages 113, 115 and is not transcribed:
> see `chinese-pages.yaml`.

## The judgment

- Ta Yew embraces Origin and Luxuriance. ~

## 彖傳 — "Wăn Wang says"

Wan Wang says:—The Soft (——) attaining tho throne of honour (the fifth stroke) and the great due Medium, and the upper and lower (strokes) responding _to it, constitutes the Ta Yew: Diagram. —Its virtus is Hard, firm, and luminously excellent; it revolves in scason responding to Heaven, and hence embraces both Origin and Luxuriance.

## 大象傳 — "Chow Kung says"

Chow Kung says :—Fire ⟦trigram figure⟧ above Heaven ⟦trigram figure⟧ constitute the Ta Yew Diagram, By it the Model Man puts a stop to evil, and causes goodness to spread abroad, (thus causing men) to obey. the "sinless nature bestowed by Heaven.

## The lines

**First-Nine.** (Represents) having no intercourse with what is injurious, and hence no injury arising; being in diflicultics, but no danger ensuing. Chow Kung says:—The First-Nine, (stroke) of the Ta Yew Diagram, (represents) not having intercourse with What is injurious. _

**Second-Nine.** (Represents) a large cart for storing - Up goods; wherever you go no injury happens (to the goods). Chow Kung says:—A large cart stowed with goods, implies hat they are piled up in the centre, so that they cankos be injured, —

**Third-Nine.** (Represents) the nobility of the first rank offering sacrifices to the Son of Heaven; mean men cannot do this, Chow Kung says :—The nobility of the first rank now make offerings to the Son of Heaven’. is the mean man injures himself (by using this stroke in divination), 7.-

**Fourth-Nine.** (Represents) freedom from haughtiness, (in which case) no dissatisfaction arises, Chow Kung says:—(The statement), Being free from haughtiness no dissatisfaction arises, implies power of discrimination.

**Fifth-Six.** Represents placing implicit confidence in others; if dignity is preserved, then good luck follows. Chow Kung says:—Placing implicit confidence in others, implies manifesting the inclinations by trusting in them.—Good luck follows a dignified demeanor, because every thing is then easy of attainment without anxious preparation.

**Sixth-Nine.** (Represents) protection from Heaven; | this is lucky, and in every respect advantageous, Chow Kung says:—The top stroke of the Ta Yow Diagram is lucky (because: it represents) protection from’ Heaven,,
