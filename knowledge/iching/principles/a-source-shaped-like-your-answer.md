# A source shaped like the answer you owe is the most dangerous kind

**The rule.** When a source happens to supply units the same shape as your own output — one line where you need one line, one word where you need one word — **treat that resemblance as a hazard rather than a convenience.** The shape is a coincidence of format; it is not evidence that the content belongs in your slot.

**When it fires.** Whenever a source can be transposed into your deliverable without being argued with.

---

## Why this holds

**The parent's [`commentary-is-not-a-rendering`](https://github.com/opencosmos-ai/taoteching) holds that a commentator's gloss is an argument for a rendering, never a rendering. That rule is easy to keep when the commentary is a paragraph and the rendering is a word.** It becomes hard exactly when the shapes match — because then there is nothing to do, no step where the judgement would have to be exercised, and the borrowing does not feel like borrowing. It feels like reading the answer off.

**Format similarity is the weakest possible evidence of semantic fit, and the most persuasive.** 雜卦 (*Zágùa*) gives sixty-four one-line glosses. `render` needs sixty-four single terms. The two are the same shape and are not the same thing: one is a Han-era mnemonic pairing hexagrams by contrast, the other is the word a player sees on a card. Nothing but the format connects them.

**And the failure would be invisible in the output.** A `render` silently taken from 雜卦 looks exactly like a `render` argued from the Chinese. There is no artefact left behind, no citation, nothing for a later reader to find — which is why the defence has to be a rule rather than a review.

**This is also how a Wing's reading gets promoted into a fact.** 說卦 (*Shuōguà*) says 乾為天 (*qián wéi tiān*) — "qian is sky". Rendering the trigram *as* its Shuogua image would install one Warring States gloss as the name every player sees, with the authority of the base text rather than of a commentary. The eight `../trigrams/*.md` files say so in their own draft notes, because that is where the temptation will actually be met.

---

## The cases

**雜卦 is named as the sleeper in the source list** — the tersest gloss the tradition gives each hexagram, and *"the closest thing in the classical corpus to the single word `render` is asking for."* → [README](../README.md#十翼--the-wings)

**說卦 and the trigram images**, where the trap is stated in full: the Wings are the oldest interpretation in existence and are still interpretation. *"Use them. Do not copy them."* → [method § 5](../method.md#5-the-wings-are-commentary)

---

## Where it does not fire

**It is not a reason to avoid the source.** 說卦 is the primary evidence for the eight trigrams and must be read closely; the rule governs what is done with it, not whether it is opened.

**And it does not reach sources that are genuinely the text.** `../sources/zhouyi/` is what a rendering answers to. The hazard is specific to *commentary* and to *other people's translations* arriving pre-shaped.

**Nor is it about length alone.** A long commentary can be quietly borrowed too; the shape is what removes the friction, not the size.

---

## What it obliges

1. **When the shapes match, add the step back.** Say what the source's unit *argues*, in your own words, before deciding what yours will be.
2. **Cite the source in the notes when it influenced the call**, so the influence is visible rather than absorbed.
3. **State the hazard where the work happens** — in the draft note of the file that will face it, not only in the method.
4. **Be most suspicious when a task suddenly looks finished.** That feeling is the symptom.
