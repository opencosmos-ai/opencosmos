# A text that arranges its own terms has already ruled on them, and the arrangement outranks the lists

**The rule.** Classical Chinese rarely defines a term; it **arranges** it. Where a text sets its
terms in a pair, a sequence, or a run of parallel slots, that arrangement is a reading the text
has already made: it says which sense of each term is in play, and it fixes every slot in the run
at **one level of abstraction**. Find the arrangements before you count instances, and weigh them
above any list of attributes the same text gives.

**When it fires.** Whenever a term has two defensible senses and the case for each is a tally.
Stop counting and go find where the text lines that term up beside its neighbours.

**The name is the case that produced it** — 說卦 (*shuōguà*, "Discussion of the Trigrams") ch 3
arranges the eight trigrams as four pairs, which settled 巽 (*xùn*). The rule is not about eight
or about four.

---

## Why this holds

**A list can hold anything; an arrangement cannot.** 說卦 ch 11 gives 巽 eleven items — 木
(*mù*, "wood"), 風 (*fēng*, "wind"), the eldest daughter, a straight cord, an artificer,
whiteness, length, height, advance and retreat, indecision, and scent. A list is a **catchment**:
it collects everything a graph has ever been made to carry, at any level, in any register, and
nothing in it is under pressure from anything else in it. An arrangement is a claim with a
**shape** — each slot has to do the same kind of work as the slot beside it and the opposite kind
of work to the slot facing it. That is why an arrangement discriminates between two senses and a
list never can. **The list says what a term may suggest; the arrangement says what it is.**

**And an arrangement rules on every slot at once, which is why it is worth more than any single
instance.** 說卦 ch 3 reads 天地定位，山澤通氣，雷風相薄，水火不相射 — *"sky and earth set the
positions; mountain and lake exchange breath; thunder and wind rouse each other; water and fire do
not quench each other."* Four pairs, eight slots, and every slot holds something you can point at
in a landscape. A candidate that would be out of register in that company is out of register
everywhere else it appears too. One 大象傳 (*dàxiàngzhuàn*) line can never deliver that; it has no
company.

**A structure the text repeats is the text agreeing with itself.** 說卦 pairs the same terms twice
in separate chapters — ch 3 above, and ch 6's 故水火相逮，雷風不相悖，山澤通氣, *"so water and fire
reach each other, thunder and wind do not oppose each other, mountain and lake exchange breath."*
An arrangement stated twice in different words is the nearest thing this corpus has to a second
witness that is not a translator, and [[no-neutral-witness]] says how rare that is here.

**It is also the defence against the count.** The parent project's rule is that *a distributional
argument is a reason to look, not a reason to conclude*, and this book will offer a great many
narrow majorities: 巽 is 風 in ten images and 木 in five, which is a majority and not a
settlement. **The arrangement is what converts a majority into a ruling** — or overturns it.

**This is the other half of [[a-closed-set-is-one-decision]], and the halves are not
interchangeable.** That rule is about the **English**: decide the set together, because the set is
what the reader meets, and the coherence of your own output is evidence. This one is about the
**Chinese**: the source has already grouped its terms, and the grouping is a ruling it made
before you arrived. The two met on 巽 — Shalom read the seven renderings already made and saw that
*wind* completes a register *wood* breaks; the text turned out to have said so in ch 3 — and when
both point the same way, the decision is as secure as this book gets. But this one fires on terms
belonging to no closed set at all, wherever the text lines its words up.

## The cases

**巽 — 風 rather than 木, decided by structure against a count.** On the tally it is 風 in ten
大象傳 and 木 in five, and choosing *wind* costs 鼎 (50) the cauldron on a **wood** fire that its
whole image is built on. What settled it is that 說卦 uses 風 every time it names the eight as a
system — ch 3's four pairs, ch 4's 雷以動之，風以散之 (*"thunder rouses them, wind scatters
them"*), ch 6's 橈萬物者，莫疾乎風 (*"nothing bends the ten thousand things faster than wind"*) —
while 木 appears in none of them, only in the ch 11 catchment beside the straight cord and the
scent. → [巽](../glossary/巽-xun.md#the-ruling--wind-shaloms-call-2026-09-12)

**兌 — the pairing killed *moisture* before the images were counted.** 澤 (*zé*) genuinely carries
moisture, lustre and 恩澤 (*ēnzé*, "beneficence"), and McClatchie's *Moisture* reads beautifully at
31 咸 山上有澤, *"moisture on the mountain"*. It dies on 山澤通氣: a term that **exchanges breath
with a height** is the hollow facing it, which is also what 47 困 澤无水 (*"the 澤 without water"*)
requires — a thing that can be empty of its own contents is a container. The pair said so before
any of the fifteen images were tallied. → [兌](../glossary/兌-dui.md#the-ruling--lake-shaloms-call-2026-09-12)

**乾 — two arrangements, and picking one ruled on all eight.** 說卦 gives every trigram an
**action** in ch 7 (乾，健也 — *"qian is vigour"*) and an **image** in ch 11 (乾為天 — *"qian is
sky"*), and both are parallel eight-slot runs. The 大象傳 bound into every hexagram runs on the
images — across the 64 they name 天 18 times, 地 17, 雷 15, 山 15, 澤 15, 水 11, 風 10, 火 10,
against 健 once, 順 twice, 入 twice, 麗 once, and 動 陷 止 說 not at all. Choosing the image run
forbade *"the Gentle"*, *"the Receptive"*, *"the Abysmal"* and *"the Joyous"* in one stroke, none
of them argued on its own. →
[乾](../glossary/乾-qian.md#the-rendering-is-the-image-not-the-action)

## Where it does not fire

**A text can hold several arrangements, and they are not rivals.** 說卦 puts the same eight in a
compass and a year (ch 5, 帝出乎震), in animals (ch 8, 乾為馬 — *"qian is the horse"*), in body
parts (ch 9, 乾為首 — *"qian is the head"*) and in kin (ch 10, 長男 長女 — eldest son, eldest
daughter). None of these corrects ch 3; each answers a different question at its own register. The
obligation is to find the arrangement that governs **the sentence you are rendering** — for the
大象傳, the images — not to find one arrangement and let it rule everywhere.

**An arrangement in the Wings is still commentary.** 說卦 is Warring States, centuries later than
the judgments it organises, and
[`commentary-is-not-a-rendering`](https://github.com/shalomormsby/taoteching) holds against it
exactly as it holds against a gloss: its pairings are an argument for a reading, never a rendering.
They are the best argument available **about the trigrams**, because the trigrams have no other
text; they are not evidence about 貞 (*zhēn*), 亨 (*hēng*) or 孚 (*fú*) in the line texts, where
周易 (*zhōuyì*) repeats itself in its own voice and should be read there first.

**Not every adjacency is an arrangement.** Two terms in one line are a pair only when the
structure makes them one — matched grammar, a stated contrary, or the same grouping recurring
elsewhere. Reading a pairing into a chance adjacency and then deriving a register from it is
[[a-source-shaped-like-your-answer]] wearing a structural hat, and it is the specific way this
rule will be abused. **The test is recurrence:** ch 3's pairs are pairs because ch 6 makes them
again.

**And an arrangement is a reason to look again, not a licence to smooth.** Where the Chinese
genuinely puts a member out of step with its neighbours,
[`divergence-stays-open`](https://github.com/shalomormsby/taoteching) and
[[never-supply-what-the-source-withheld]] still govern: record the strain rather than choosing the
English that tidies it. 60 節 澤上有水 (*"water above the lake"*) reads oddly in English because it
reads oddly in Chinese, and that oddity is kept.

---

## What it obliges

1. **Before ruling on a term, collect every place the text arranges it** — pairs, sequences,
   parallel runs — and read those before the attribute lists and before the instance counts.
2. **Name the arrangement that governs the sentence you are rendering**, and rule on it once for
   the whole run rather than once per member.
3. **Weigh a grouping the text repeats above one it states once**, and say in the entry where the
   repetition is.
4. **Quote the arrangement, with its chapter, in the entry.** A ruling that rests on structure is
   only checkable by a later reader if the structure is on the page.
