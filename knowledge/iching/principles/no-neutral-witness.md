# Where every available witness shares a bias, measure across them — excluding them buys blindness, not purity

**The rule.** Before excluding a source for its overlay, ask what the field looks like without it. **If every available witness carries the same bias, exclusion does not remove the bias — it removes the ability to see it.** Vendor them, label the overlay, and make it something measured across witnesses rather than taken on trust.

**When it fires.** When a source is about to be ruled out on grounds of contamination rather than of rights or accuracy.

---

## Why this holds

**Excluding a biased source only helps if a less biased one takes its place.** Where one exists, exclusion is straightforwardly right. Where none does, the bias does not leave the work — it moves from the page, where it is legible and arguable, into the collaborator's memory of what these texts "usually say," where it is neither.

**Bias is only correctable when it is countable.** With three translations in the repository, the overlay is a quantity: 君子 (*jūnzǐ*) is *"the superior man"* in one and *"the Model Man"* in another, and the distance between them is the size of the decision. With none, the reader has nothing to compare against, and the first English phrase that comes to mind arrives with no competition.

**And a catalogued bias is a usable source.** What Legge does wrong is known, finite, and a century old: 天 (*tiān*) → "Heaven", 王 (*wáng*) → "the king", 德 (*dé*) → "virtue". Three of those are forbidden outright by the vendored glossary. The dangerous bias is the *un*catalogued one — and the surest way to leave a bias uncatalogued is to refuse to look at it.

**The specific fact that produced this rule is worth stating plainly.** The Tao Te Ching has eight public-domain English translations, so that project can triangulate and a single wanderer shows up against the others. The I Ching has three, and **every one of them is missionary work** — Legge for the London Missionary Society, de Harlez a Catholic monsignor, McClatchie an Anglican who argues in his own Appendix that 神 (*shén*) *means* God. There is no clean alternative to reach for. This directory's earlier stance excluded Legge on overlay grounds and bought no purity by it, only working blind.

## Where it does not fire

**It is not an argument against exclusion as such.** Where a genuinely less contaminated source exists, prefer it. The parent project consults eight and needs no such rule.

**It never touches rights.** A source that fails the admission rules is out regardless of how the field looks without it.

**And it does not weaken the locks.** Reading Legge for construal and never for a word is what makes vendoring him safe; the compensating discipline is not optional, it is the condition.

---

## What it obliges

1. **Survey the field before excluding**, and write down what it contains — see [[record-the-search]].
2. **State the bias on the page**, in the file's own header, where a reader meets it before the text.
3. **Name the compensating discipline** and make it specific enough to follow.
4. **Prefer measuring to avoiding**: put the competing renderings side by side where the decision will be made.
